# Explanatory 3D scene

The local project retains horizon tables and provenance, but no usable georeferenced DEM raster is present. The displayed procedural terrain is **illustrative, not a site reconstruction**. Its geometry does not determine scientific visibility, shadows or power. Only spacecraft geometry casts illustrative contact/self-shadows. Terrain and rocks do not cast blocking shadows. No atmosphere, relays or orbital motion are simulated.

Local east/north/up maps to Three.js **+X/−Z/+Y**, a right-handed frame. For azimuth `a` clockwise from north and elevation `e`, the unit direction is `(cos(e) sin(a), sin(e), −cos(e) cos(a))`. The panel's outward normal uses the engine's azimuth and elevation `90° − tilt`. Mesh dimensions, Sun/Earth apparent sizes, distances and lander placement are illustrative. A white directional light follows the actual Sun vector. It is suppressed for blocked or uncertain source classifications, with restrained indirect fill for readability. Rendering is not an irradiance or electrical model.

The scene receives the current evaluated request and `rows[state.sourceIndex]` from the existing playback adapter. The engine's clearance classification and estimated power remain authoritative in the numerical panel. The camera uses OrbitControls with bounded zoom and keyboard equivalents. Camera interaction does not change source time, the operation or its deadline.

Rendering happens on data, camera or size changes rather than a permanent animation loop. Pixel ratio is capped at 1.5. Hidden pages do not render; source playback already stops when hidden. Page teardown disposes geometry, materials, controls, renderer, observers and registered listeners. Context loss displays a reload instruction; unavailable WebGL or failed local module imports leave numerical results and charts usable.

Dependency: locally vendored Three.js 0.170.0 and its OrbitControls, with MIT license retained. No backend or framework build step was added. Serve with `python -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory docs` and open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/mission-console.html`.

Implementation is untested: no tests, browser automation, verification builds or screenshots were run for this change. The scene does not independently validate the scientific model.

Visual upgrade: original procedural regolith/roughness/foil maps, shallow craters, small instanced rocks, metallic struts and footpads, thermal blankets, dish antenna and framed solar cells. Earth uses fixed illustrative procedural continent silhouettes, not NASA imagery or a time-varying orientation product. Sun/Earth proxies are not to scale; edge labels identify off-screen directions. Click labels for object details; Focus lander changes only the camera. No additional dependencies or external assets were added.

Panel explanation: three parallel amber arrows travel opposite the sampled unit Sun vector, toward the actual oriented panel. Above-terrain samples use solid guides; blocked samples suppress them; uncertain samples use dashed guides that do not confirm illumination. Back-facing panels are identified explicitly. Guides do not raycast against decorative terrain or calculate power.

Show geometry reveals a local horizontal reference, vertical and outward-normal arrows, and tilt/azimuth/incidence arcs. Tilt is the plane angle to horizontal, shown equivalently between the vertical and panel normal. Azimuth is clockwise north-to-east in the horizontal plane. Incidence is acos of the clamped normal/Sun dot product, using the same ENU convention as the engine. Collinear vectors have no unique arc plane; their numeric angle remains displayed. Horizontal panels have no defined normal azimuth; the entered azimuth is retained and identified separately. An always-visible local-horizontal ring and N/E markers are references, not sampled terrain horizons.

Anchored labels report engine-estimated power, demand and balance, and source Sun/Earth elevations with engine terrain classifications. Narrow viewports use compact object buttons with the same values below the scene. Expanded diagnostics separate azimuth, elevation and sampled terrain clearance. Scene scale, ray length, terrain, spacecraft shape, shadow rendering, and Earth appearance remain illustrative. Rendering withheld near the uncertain horizon does not replace the engine's numerical estimate. No new astronomical interpolation, power calculation, source data or sensitivity assumptions were introduced. Layout and visual appearance have not been verified in a browser for this change.

Spatial correction: the earlier bodies were foreground spheres only 18 scene units from the lander, with depth testing disabled. Their camera-dependent parallax did not match the vanishing direction of parallel sunlight rays, even though the underlying ENU Sun vectors agreed. The bodies now live in a separate sky scene rendered first with a camera at the sky origin. Only the foreground camera's orientation, aspect and field of view are shared; its translation and orbit distance never enter sky placement. The sky radius and disc radii define illustrative angular sizes, not measured Sun/Earth distances or diameters. Foreground geometry renders after the sky; any decorative occlusion is not evidence of scientific blockage. An always-readable outlined direction marker and classification remain independent of decorative terrain.

Each actual source sample supplies one normalized observer-to-Sun vector s in world ENU. The sky Sun is placed on +s; the directional light sits at its target plus 24*s, emitting toward -s. Each guide ends at a distinct panel-local point transformed into world space: its upstream start is p + L*s and its direction is -s. The incidence arc uses the outward world-space panel normal and the same s. Sky labels project direction using camera rotation only. Body directions are never adjusted to fit the viewport; off-screen arrows and behind-view indicators are UI markers only.

Blocked samples hide the ordinary celestial disc and show a labeled crossed direction marker. Uncertain samples also hide the disc and use a dashed question marker; uncertain sunlight guides remain dashed and direct lighting withheld. Above-terrain samples allow the sky disc, retain the source-based classification and do not imply adequate electrical power. Earth labels include its actual elevation and azimuth. The fixed procedural surface markings remain illustrative; elevation is not axial tilt. No astronomical interpolation, Earth orientation product or new visibility calculation was added.

Reset view uses a lower, horizon-oriented camera. Focus lander, Focus panel, Look toward Sun and Look toward Earth change only camera pose/target. A subtle direction-only sky line marks the local horizontal, not a sampled terrain skyline. Both render passes remain event-driven, using one existing renderer, without a new animation loop. Sky geometry, materials and textures participate in the existing disposal path.

Tests and browser verification were not run at the user's request. Visual correctness has not been confirmed in a running browser.

Exposure and material colors/metalness were adjusted for legibility without modifying any electrical inputs or power formulas. Sunlight is now represented by a translucent, depth-tested gold planar band and parallel line paths in world ENU, ending at the panel's front/back surface according to its actual orientation. This continuation is an explanatory overlay; it is not a terrain-occlusion calculation. When the Sun is behind the camera, the continuation is hidden and the existing behind-view marker remains. Blocked samples suppress all sunlight paths; uncertain samples show dashed lines without a filled band. An illuminated-looking panel still does not establish adequate electrical power.
